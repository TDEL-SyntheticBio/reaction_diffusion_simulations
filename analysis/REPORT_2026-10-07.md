# JAPI engine and pipeline check, 7 October 2026

Everything here was computed with the unmodified engine at commit `07a328b` through the wrappers in
`analysis/`, with explicit seeds, and re-verified by independent review passes (twelve on the engine
conventions, three on the analysis code). Engine conventions are in `CLAUDE.md`; this file holds the
results. All runs are 100×100 unless stated, dt = 0.01, frames every t = 10. "Synchronous" is the
Supp Table 1 Fig 1C protocol (random_uniform_over0, spike 1.0, no nucleation); "nucleation" is the
Fig 2L/3 protocol (all_off, spike 2.0, nucleation 0.01, kick 4.0).

## 1. What was checked first, and why

The handoff's spatial statistics came from throwaway scripts that no longer exist, its component
counter in the deposit uses the mirror of the engine's adjacency, and every spatial statement was quoted
in units of an inhibitor range that had never been measured on the lattice. So before any screen the
measurement pipeline was rebuilt on the engine's own neighbour arrays and run against known answers,
and the inhibitor range was measured directly on arrested domains.

## 7. What this changes in the handoff and the session notes

1. λ_i for every 2D statement is √(1.5·D_i/γ): 5.5 cells at D_i = 10, 7.7 at D_i = 20.
2. "The hole sits at 2λ_i" was a coincidence with the understated λ. Measured holes: 8.9, 9.7, 11.0,
   16.3 (D_i = 10) and 9.1, 12.2 (D_i = 20) against 2λ = 11.0 and 15.5.
3. "Irregularity comes from asynchronous initiation" rested on counting transient kicks as spots. Under
   the published nucleation protocol the domains are ordered and frozen from t ≈ 50.
4. "Each domain is a deterministic object with a parameter-set width" holds for b_i ≥ 11 on the Fig 1C
   line, not for the regular-spots panel, whose spots self-replicate.
5. The handoff's hex classifier label "pure arrest" for b_i 4–12 covers two mechanisms.
6. The Fig 1C irregular-spots regime is reachable under continuous nucleation (55 small domains), but
   only visible with a kick-independent threshold.
7. Component counts in the deposit's `npz_feature_distribution.py` used mirrored adjacency; thin
   structures fragment (1 → 16 components on a diagonal line).

## 8. Open items, in order

1. **Reclassify the published grid with the isolated-seed test** on top of the closed-form classifier:
   for each non-Turing set, does one seed arrest, divide, or spread? The three-way split above is
   likely the structure Reviewer 2 wants behind "irregular". Cost: ~1 min per set on one core at
   141×141 to t = 600; sample the grid.
2. **Peak statistics need more seeds.** Three seeds of a 17-domain pattern give a hole but no
   significant peak. Ten seeds, or 200×200 grids, for the sparse panels before quoting second-shell
   order, and then the hard-disc or Gibbs null the handoff proposed.
3. **Experiment-side segmentation**: the same threshold trap exists in images where fresh activation
   events are bright. Fixed level plus persistence across frames, and a size cut set from the domain
   distribution, carry over directly.
4. **Density under nucleation** is set by the race between nucleation rate and halo growth; a sweep of
   `nucleation_rate` at the 2L set (0.001–0.03) would give the density law and the prediction that
   pulsed induction changes density, not order.
5. The JA/JAPI size-uniformity comparison needs matched time points (JA is all-on by t = 50 here).

### Nucleation, stopping criterion and frames

- Kick rate on 100×100 at rate 0.01: 0.94 kicks per step while most cells are off; 0.04 on the
  20×20 Fig 3L grid; 0.56 in the labyrinth run (44% of cells above a_ss). A single-cell kick
  decays below 0.3·r_a within ~0.5 time units.
- Under nucleation the window diff per cell is ≥ 0.05 (median 0.13 on 100×100, 0.10 on 20×20), so
  the 1e-4 stopping criterion never fires. Without nucleation (Fig 1C protocol, 50,000 max): all-on
  (b_i = 1) and all-off (b_i = 14) stopped at steps 2,000–2,200; stripes, regular and irregular
  spots (b_i = 3, 5, 12) ran the full 50,000 with final window diffs 1.7e-3, 1.2e-3, 4.5e-4.
- Frame k ≥ 1 is the state after (k−1)·save_every + 1 Euler steps; the deposit's "final" frame of a
  5,000-step run is at step 4800 (t 48.01), of a 50,000-step run at 49,800.

### (a) Does the lone-seed fate survive crowding?

- **b_i = 5 (lone seed: ring, fragmentation, spreading colony).** Yes, and the colonies do fill the
  field when packed: 9, 36 and 144 seeds all reach coverage 0.243–0.247, the same as the random
  protocol, within t = 300. They do not jam into a sparse arrested pattern. What the seed count
  changes is the texture, not the amount: from few seeds the field keeps labyrinthine remnants of
  the expanding rings (largest blobs 150–250 cells against 41 for the random start) and ends with
  fewer, larger domains (92–104 blobs of ~50 cells against 167 of 30), and it is still fragmenting
  and coarsening at t = 600 (blob counts step by 1–3 per frame, cells drift ±10–70 per 100 t). The
  single colony itself reaches 0.233 by t = 600 and is still growing at 7 cells per time unit.
- **b_i = 9 (lone seed: 37-cell disc, static to t ≈ 300, then divides).** Partly. Dense fields do not
  divide at all: with 144 seeds the blob count is fixed from t = 40 (99–108 blobs, one per surviving
  seed) and the random start fixes 130–137 blobs by t = 210–390; coverage saturates at 0.10–0.11 and
  domains are 17–21 cells, smaller than the isolated 37. Sparse fields do what the lone seed does,
  but much earlier: with 9 seeds dumbbells appear before t = 150 and the count rises 9 → 17 → 33 → 58
  by t = 600, coverage 0.077 and still rising; with 36 seeds 33 → 70, coverage 0.09, slowing. The lone
  seed's division is a linear instability of the disc. Its convergence diagnostic first falls to
  2e-10 at t = 100 (the disc is a numerical fixed point) and then grows exponentially at
  σ = 0.071 per time unit (doubling time 10) until the split at t ≈ 350. Started from round-off that
  takes 300 time units; started from a neighbour's halo it takes under 100. The lone-seed division
  time is therefore a property of the perturbation level, not of the parameters, and crowding
  suppresses the instability altogether above roughly 100 domains per 141×141.
- **b_i = 12 (lone seed: 19-cell arrested domain at a fixed point).** Yes, completely. Every seed
  becomes one arrested domain, nothing replicates, and the blob count equals the number of seeds that
  did not merge or fall inside a neighbour's halo: 9 → 8–9, 36 → 32–33, 144 → 90–97. Domain size is
  the isolated size or a little above it when sparse (19–24 cells, with cells ON creeping by under
  half a cell per domain per 100 t, origin unexplained) and below it when crowded (14 cells at 144
  seeds). Blob counts are fixed by t = 10–40.

### (b) Settling time

Under the strict definition (blob count and cells ON both unchanged from some frame through
t = 600) only the b_i = 12 lone seed settles, at t = 10; every field run keeps creeping in cells ON by a
few cells per frame even when its blob count has been constant for hundreds of time units, so all
46 field runs read "not settled" (two reach it only on the final two frames). The blob count alone
settles as follows (`settle_blobs_t` in the CSV):

|---|---|---|

| 5 | 9 / 36 / 144 seeds | never or t = 450–590 |
| 5 | random | t = 170, 280, 560 |

The published convergence criterion (change per cell-field below 1e-4 over a 200-step window,
after 1,000 steps; `CLAUDE.md` §8) was evaluated on every window of every run. It never fires for any
field run: the synchronous fields sit at 4e-4 to 2e-3 for the whole 600 time units and the nucleation
runs at 5e-2 to 8e-2, so the published 50,000-step cap, not convergence, is what would have ended
them, at t = 500, while the b_i = 5 seeded fields and the b_i = 9 sparse fields were still changing. It
does fire for the two lone seeds: b_i = 12 at t = 16 (correct, a fixed point) and **b_i = 9 at t = 22,
which is 330 time units before the domain divides**. A convergence stop on a lone seed in this regime
certifies a transient.

### (c) Do the two field protocols land in the same place?

- At b_i = 5 the random start and continuous nucleation agree on coverage (0.246 and 0.259) but not
  on texture: 167 blobs of 30 cells against 139 real domains of 36 cells, and the nucleation field is
  never static (one kick per step). Neither matches the seeded runs' domain counts; coverage is the
  only invariant of this regime.
- At b_i = 9 the dense conditions agree with each other: random 133 blobs at coverage 0.110, nucleation
  126 real domains at 0.122, 144 seeds 103 at 0.105; the sparse seeded fields are below this and still
  rising at t = 600.
- At b_i = 12 nothing is shared but the domain: the random start nucleates 28–31 domains (coverage
  0.03), continuous nucleation 112 (0.08), and seeded fields exactly their seed count. In the arrest
  regime the field is the list of successful nucleation events; the protocol sets how long that list
  is and the parameters set only the size of each entry.

In short: the lone-seed fate predicts the field's mechanism class and its invariant. In the
replicating regime the invariant is coverage, reached from any seed count, while domain count and
morphology stay history-dependent and unsettled at t = 600; in the arrest regime the invariant is the
domain size, and the field is its seeds. At the boundary (b_i = 9) crowding decides which of the two
the field behaves like.
